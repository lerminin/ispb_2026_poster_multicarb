## Poster abstract

**Multiple carbapenemases in Enterobacterales**

Nicole Lerminiaux<sup>1,2</sup>, Ken Fakharuddin<sup>2</sup>, Erin McGill<sup>3</sup>, Yves Longtin<sup>4</sup>, Laura Mataseje<sup>2</sup>, the CNISP CPO working group, the NEKSUS consortium, Nicole Stoesser<sup>1,5</sup>, Dorottya Nagy<sup>1,5</sup>, Samuel Lipworth<sup>1,5</sup>

<sup>1</sup> Nuffield Department of Medicine, University of Oxford, Oxford, Oxfordshire, United Kingdom
<sup>2</sup> National Microbiology Laboratory, Public Health Agency of Canada, Winnipeg, Manitoba, Canada
<sup>3</sup> Public Health Agency of Canada, Ottawa, Ontario, Canada
<sup>4</sup> Jewish General Hospital, Montréal, Québec, Canada
<sup>5</sup> NIHR Health Protection Research Unit in Healthcare Associated Infections and Antimicrobial Resistance, University of Oxford, Oxford, Oxfordshire, United Kingdom

Carbapenemase genes confer resistance to last-resort treatment options for multi-drug-resistant Enterobacterales infections. Carbapenemases are frequently carried on plasmids and transposons that facilitate their mobility. Carbapenemase families (NDM, OXA, KPC, VIM, IMP) have varying hydrolytic profiles and inhibitor susceptibilities and therefore require different clinical treatment strategies; however, the emergence of isolates encoding multiple carbapenemase genes is complicating treatment by further limiting effective antimicrobials. The genomic and plasmid context of the multiple carbapenemases producers has not yet been explored at scale. To characterize the genomic features and plasmids of multiple carbapenemase isolates, we analyzed Enterobacterales isolates carrying multiple carbapenemase genes in two national surveillance collections (United Kingdom NEKSUS n=24 and Canada CNISP n= 90) and from the public plasmid database PLSDB (n=99). Most isolates were *Klebsiella pneumoniae* (51.2 %, 109/213) or *Escherichia coli* (20.2 %, 43/213). Each isolate encoded resistance genes corresponding to an average of 9 antimicrobial classes (range: 1 - 15), and isolates had an average of 5 plasmids (range: 1 - 14). Most isolates had all carbapenemases located on a single plasmid (52.6 %, 112/213), followed by two plasmids (36.2 %, 77/213), one plasmid and the chromosome (7.5 %, 16/213), or three plasmids (3.8 %, 8/213). Half of isolates carried two carbapenemase types (49.8 %, 106/213), and the remaining half carried multiple copies of the same carbapenemase (47.9 %, 102/213) or different alleles of the same carbapenemase (2.3 %, 5/213). NDM/OXA co-carriage was the most common carbapenemase profile in the surveillance datasets (63.2 %, 72/114), whereas multiple KPC copies (42.4 %, 42/99), multiple NDM copies (18.1 %, 18/99), or KPC/NDM (13.1 %, 13/99) were most common in isolates linked to PLSDB plasmids. Most isolates with multiple copies of the same carbapenemase on one plasmid had tandem repeats (53.9 %, 41/76), about half of which were mediated by IS26/IS15DIV (48.8 %, 20/41). Clustering all plasmids from these isolates (n=1004) with pling resulted in 130 communities and 446 subcommunities, with subcommunities containing carbapenemase plasmids (n=139) representing a distinct subset of AMR plasmid subcommunities (n=232). This study provides valuable insights into the genomic features of these isolates to further our understanding of their epidemiology.
